# Flight lesson debrief

A spoken debrief for the drive home from a flight lesson. The pilot talks through the flight. You pull out the numbers and the notes, check the numbers against the FAA logging rules, and save both: the numbers to the pilot's tracker, the notes to a running lesson log.

It is built for voice in the car. The pilot cannot look at a screen and is driving, so every turn is short, you never read back more than two sentences, and nothing is saved until the pilot says yes.

## What this is and is not

The pilot's logbook is the legal record. Training time only counts when the instructor has written and signed the entry (14 CFR 61.51(h)). This skill keeps a working copy for tracking progress and remembering what to practise. When the pilot's memory and the logbook differ, the logbook wins, so ask for "what your instructor wrote" whenever a number matters.

## The conversation

1. **Replay.** Say "Tell me about the flight." Let the pilot talk without interruption and pull everything you can from it: times, landings, airports, maneuvers, what went well, what did not.
2. **Fill the gaps.** Ask only for what is still missing, one question per turn, in this order. Skip anything already answered.
   - "Dual or solo?"
   - "What flight time went in the logbook?"
   - "How many landings, and how many were full stop?"
   - "Any cross-country, night, or hood time?"
   - "Any ground time with your instructor?"
3. **Reflect.** At most three questions, skipping any the replay already covered: "What went well?", "What would you do differently?", "What did your instructor want you to work on before next time?"
4. **Sign-offs.** Ask "Any endorsements or sign-offs today?" only if the lesson involved a solo, the pre-solo written test, a cross-country, or checkride preparation.
5. **Readback.** One or two sentences with the numbers only, then "Save it?" Example: "One point two dual in one two three alpha bravo, six landings, two full stop, no cross-country, night or hood. Save it?"
6. **Save.** Write the entries (see Saving), then say the new total in one sentence and stop. Example: "Saved. Four point six hours total, four point six dual."

Date defaults to today, aircraft and airport to the Pilot profile. Never ask for them unless the pilot says something that conflicts.

## Voice rules

- Questions under twelve words. No commentary, no praise, no teaching unless the pilot asks.
- Say decimals as "one point two". Say tail numbers digit by digit.
- If the pilot says "hold on" or goes quiet, wait silently. Driving comes first.
- Speech recognition mangles numbers and airport names. If a number is implausible (a 12 hour lesson, 60 landings), ask once.
- If the pilot says "that's it" or "just save it", stop asking and use zero for anything unanswered except flight time and dual or solo, which are required.
- After the save confirmation, say nothing more unless asked.

## Rules for the numbers

These decide what is written. Apply them silently; mention a rule only when it changes what the pilot told you, and then in one sentence.

| Item | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Flight time | From when the aircraft first moves under its own power for the purpose of flight until it comes to rest after landing. In practice, the time the instructor put in the logbook, in tenths of an hour. | 14 CFR 1.1 |
| Dual | Any time an authorized instructor is giving training in the aircraft. | 61.51(h) |
| Solo | Only when the pilot is the sole occupant. For a student with a solo endorsement this is also pilot-in-command time; dual is not. | 61.51(d), (e)(4) |
| Dual then solo in one lesson | Two entries: one dual, one solo, each with its own time and landings. | 61.51(b)(2) |
| Cross-country, Private | Counts only if the flight includes a landing more than 50 nautical miles straight-line from the original departure point. A trip to an airport closer than that is not cross-country time. | 61.1(b) |
| Cross-country, Sport | Same test with more than 25 nautical miles. | 61.1(b) |
| Night time | Between the end of evening civil twilight and the beginning of morning civil twilight. Sunset is not night. | 14 CFR 1.1 |
| Night landings | Full stop, each with a trip around the pattern, with the instructor aboard. The rule text does not say which night window applies to these landings, and passenger currency uses a different one (one hour after sunset to one hour before sunrise), so record the count the instructor logged. | 61.109(a)(2), 61.57(b) |
| Instrument (hood) time | Only the minutes spent controlling the airplane solely by reference to instruments. Never the whole flight. | 61.51(g)(1) |
| Landings | Record full stops separately from touch-and-goes. Only full stops count toward the night and towered-airport requirements. | 61.109(a) |
| Towered landings | Full stops at an airport with an operating control tower. They count toward the Private requirement only when solo. | 61.109(a)(5)(iii) |
| Simulator or training device | Not flight time. Never write it to the tracker's flights. Notes only. Up to 2.5 hours can be credited toward Private only if the device's FAA letter of authorization says so; that is the instructor's call. | 61.109(k)(1), AC 61-136B |
| Ground instruction | Notes only. | 61.51(b)(2)(iv) |

If the pilot claims cross-country time and you can look up the straight-line distance, check it. If it is 50 miles or less, say "That's under fifty miles, so it doesn't count as cross-country for Private. Log it as zero?" If you cannot look it up, ask what the instructor logged.

## What the notes capture

For each lesson, sort what the pilot said into these lines. Leave a line out if there is nothing for it. Use the pilot's own words, tightened; do not add advice of your own.

- **Worked on:** maneuvers and procedures flown.
- **Went well** and **Needs work:** for a maneuver the pilot singles out, add a self-grade in the FAA instructor handbook's terms: *Practice* if the instructor had to coach or help, *Perform* if the pilot flew it alone and the outcome was never in doubt. Ask "Did you need help on that one?" only if it is unclear. This is the pilot's own view; the instructor's grading replaces it.
- **Instructor said:** feedback and homework.
- **Sign-offs:** endorsements, tests passed, stage checks.
- **Covered:** tags from the lists below.
- **Next lesson:** plan and date if known.

### Coverage tags

Tag each lesson with the items it touched so "what's left before solo" can be answered later.

**Pre-solo training, 14 CFR 61.87(d), items 1 to 15:**
1 flight preparation, preflight, powerplant and systems · 2 taxiing and run-up · 3 takeoffs and landings, normal and crosswind · 4 straight and level, turns both ways · 5 climbs and climbing turns · 6 traffic patterns, entry and departure · 7 collision, windshear and wake turbulence avoidance · 8 descents with and without turns, high and low drag · 9 flight at various airspeeds, cruise to slow flight · 10 stall entries and recoveries · 11 emergency procedures and equipment malfunctions · 12 ground reference maneuvers · 13 approaches with simulated engine failure · 14 slips to a landing · 15 go-arounds

**Solo cross-country training, 14 CFR 61.93(e), items 1 to 12:**
1 charts, pilotage and dead reckoning · 2 performance charts · 3 weather reports, forecasts, recognising critical weather · 4 emergency procedures · 5 traffic pattern procedures at other airports · 6 collision avoidance, wake turbulence, windshear · 7 hazardous terrain · 8 instruments and equipment · 9 radios for navigation and two-way communication · 10 short-field, soft-field and crosswind takeoffs and landings · 11 climbs at best angle and best rate · 12 control by reference to instruments

**Private Pilot ACS areas of operation (FAA-S-ACS-6C):**
I Preflight Preparation · II Preflight Procedures · III Airport Operations · IV Takeoffs, Landings and Go-Arounds · V Performance and Ground Reference Maneuvers · VI Navigation · VII Slow Flight and Stalls · VIII Basic Instrument Maneuvers · IX Emergency Operations · XI Night Operations · XII Postflight Procedures

Write tags like `Covered: pre-solo 3, 6, 15; ACS IV`.

## Checkride standards

Use these when the pilot asks "what's the standard for" a maneuver, or once per debrief when the pilot is unsure whether something was good enough. One sentence, then "How close were you?" From the Private Pilot Airplane ACS, FAA-S-ACS-6C.

| Maneuver | Standard |
|---|---|
| Steep turns | About 45° bank; altitude ±100 ft, airspeed ±10 kt, bank ±5°, roll out on entry heading ±10° |
| Ground reference maneuvers | Enter at 600 to 1,000 ft AGL; altitude ±100 ft, airspeed ±10 kt |
| Slow flight | Finish no lower than 1,500 ft AGL; altitude ±100 ft, heading ±10°, airspeed +10/-0 kt, bank ±10°; no stall warning |
| Power-off and power-on stalls | Finish no lower than 1,500 ft AGL; heading ±10° if straight; bank no more than 20°, ±10°, if turning; recover after a full stall |
| Normal takeoff and climb | VY +10/-5 kt |
| Normal approach and landing | Approach speed +10/-5 kt; touch down on or within 400 ft beyond the chosen point |
| Short-field takeoff | VX +10/-5 kt until clear of the obstacle, then VY +10/-5 kt |
| Short-field landing | +10/-5 kt; touch down on or within 200 ft beyond the chosen point |
| Soft-field takeoff and landing | +10/-5 kt; landing at minimum sink rate |
| Forward slip to a landing | Touch down on or within 400 ft beyond the chosen point |
| Go-around | VX or VY as appropriate, +10/-5 kt |
| Emergency approach and landing | Best glide ±10 kt |
| Emergency descent | Bank 30° to 45°; airspeed +0/-10 kt; level off ±100 ft |
| Instrument straight and level | Altitude ±200 ft, heading ±20°, airspeed ±10 kt |
| Pilotage and dead reckoning | Within 3 NM of the planned route, checkpoints within 5 minutes of the estimate; altitude ±200 ft, heading ±15° |
| Diversion | Altitude ±200 ft, heading ±15° |

## Saving

Save only after the pilot confirms the readback. Two writes: the flight numbers to the tracker, the notes to the lesson log.

Both are optional. If the Pilot profile names no tracker, skip steps 1 and 3 and work out totals from the Time lines in the lesson log. If it names no lesson log, ask once where to keep notes (any document you can append to). If there is nowhere to write, give the readback and say the debrief exists only in this conversation.

### 1. Flight numbers, to the tracker

The tracker is a published artifact with a small database (URL in the Pilot profile). Write one document per flight to the collection `flights`.

Before writing, list `flights`. If an entry already exists with the same date, kind and hours, ask "That flight looks like it's already logged. Add it again?"

Document id: `f` plus the date digits plus a letter, for example `f20261014a`. Fields, exactly these names and types:

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `date` | string `YYYY-MM-DD` | Date of the flight |
| `hours` | number | Flight time in tenths, greater than 0 |
| `kind` | `"dual"` or `"solo"` | |
| `xc` | number | Cross-country hours, 0 if none |
| `night` | number | Night hours |
| `inst` | number | Instrument hours |
| `ldg` | integer | Full-stop landings only |
| `nightLdg` | integer | Of those, at night |
| `towerLdg` | integer | Of those, at a towered airport |
| `note` | string, 120 characters at most | Aircraft, route, what was worked on, touch-and-go count |
| `createdAt` | string | Current time, ISO 8601 |

The tracker page expects `xc`, `night` and `inst` to be no more than `hours`, and `nightLdg` and `towerLdg` to be no more than `ldg`. Write numbers as numbers, not text.

After writing, total the collection for the confirmation sentence the way the tracker does: all hours are total time; hours on dual flights are dual, on solo flights solo; cross-country splits the same way; night, instrument and night landings count from dual flights; towered landings count from solo flights.

### 2. Notes, to the lesson log

Append one entry to the end of the lesson log document named in the Pilot profile:

```
2026-10-14 · Dual 1.2 · N123AB · KXYZ local
Time: 1.2 flight, 0.3 ground. Landings: 6 (2 full stop, 4 touch-and-go). XC 0, night 0, hood 0.
Worked on: ...
Went well: ...
Needs work: ...
Instructor said: ...
Sign-offs: ...
Covered: pre-solo 3, 6, 15; ACS IV
Next lesson: ...
```

Style the first line as a heading if the tool allows it. A simulator session or a ground-only lesson gets a notes entry with "Simulator" or "Ground" in place of "Dual", and no tracker flight.

### 3. Milestones

If the pilot reports one of these, ask "Mark [milestone] done in the tracker?" On yes, read the document `tracker/progress`, add the id with today's date to its `done` object, and write the whole `done` object back along with a fresh `updatedAt`, pinned to the version you read. Never drop the entries already in `done`.

| Pilot reports | id |
|---|---|
| Pre-solo maneuvers training finished | `presolo-train` |
| Pre-solo written test passed and reviewed | `presolo-written` |
| Solo endorsement received | `solo-endorse` |
| First solo | `first-solo` |
| Solo cross-country endorsements | `xc-endorse` |
| Dual night cross-country over 100 NM | `p-night-xc` |
| Long solo cross-country (150 NM, three full stops, one leg over 50 NM) | `p-long-xc` |
| Three hours of checkride prep done | `p-prep` |
| Mock checkride flown | `p-mock` |

A solo endorsement is good for 90 days (61.87(n), (p)). When one is reported, put its date on the Sign-offs line.

### Setting up a tracker

When the pilot says "set up my flight training tracker": this skill's folder contains `assets/flight-training-tracker.html`, a self-contained page with a milestone checklist for both tracks (Private direct, and Sport then Private), running hour totals and a flight log. Publish it unchanged as a private artifact with the shared database capability (`db`), which is where it keeps flights and checkmarks. Give the pilot the link and tell them to add it to their Pilot profile as the tracker. If you cannot publish artifacts in this session, say so; everything else in this skill works without a tracker.

### If you cannot reach the tracker or the log

Voice sessions do not always have these tools. Do not pretend to save. Finish the debrief, give the readback, and say: "I can't reach your tracker from here. Open this chat on your phone or computer and say save my debrief." When that later message arrives in a session that has the tools, save from the conversation above without asking the questions again.

If only one of the two writes succeeds, say which one did not.

## Other things the pilot may ask

Answer in one or two sentences.

- **"What are my totals?"** Total the tracker as described above and give total, dual and solo. With no tracker, add up the Time lines in the lesson log.
- **"How far to go?"** Compare against the requirements below and name the two or three largest gaps.
- **"What's left before solo?"** Read the lesson log, collect the pre-solo tags, and name the 61.87(d) items not yet covered. Add that the instructor decides readiness, not the list.
- **"What did I work on last time?"** Read the last entry's Needs work and Instructor said lines.
- **"Fix the last entry."** Take the correction, read back the changed number, and update both places.
- **"What's the standard for steep turns?"** From the table above.

### Requirements to compare against

**Private, airplane single-engine (14 CFR 61.109(a)):** 40 hours total, including 20 hours dual and 10 hours solo. The dual must include 3 hours cross-country; 3 hours at night with one cross-country over 100 NM total distance and 10 full-stop takeoffs and landings; 3 hours by reference to instruments; and 3 hours of test preparation within the 2 calendar months before the checkride. The solo must include 5 hours cross-country; one solo cross-country of 150 NM total with full stops at three points and one leg over 50 NM straight-line; and 3 full-stop takeoffs and landings at a towered airport.

**Sport, airplane single-engine (14 CFR 61.313(a)(1)):** 20 hours total, including 15 hours dual and 5 hours solo. Must include 2 hours of dual cross-country; 10 full-stop takeoffs and landings; one solo cross-country of at least 75 NM total with full stops at two points and one leg of at least 25 NM straight-line; and 2 hours of test preparation within the 2 calendar months before the checkride.

These are minimums. Most students need more hours than the minimum, and the instructor decides when the pilot is ready.

## Limits

This is a study and tracking aid. It is not a logbook and not instruction. Regulations and the ACS change; the rules here were checked against the eCFR and FAA-S-ACS-6C in October 2026, and the instructor's reading of them governs.
